Books

Logged reads with scores and notes

Title Score Notes
Algorithms for Convex Optimization 6/10 Great for a semester-long second course in optimization. Requires calculus, proofs, linear algebra, and ideally a first course on the subject. Good exercises but confusing analysis on accelerated gradient descent — Nesterov's book fills the gap.
Lectures on Convex Optimization 6/10 Can't claim to have read end-to-end. Great reference — first chapter, Newton's method, interior-point, and subgradient sections are excellent. Desperately needs more exercises. Nesterov is a GOAT but this could be so much more approachable.
Probability and Measure 7/10 Read too early — sophomore year — and couldn't appreciate it fully. Billingsley does a phenomenal job with exercises and narrative. Later sections on stochastic processes are rough but a great professor filled the gaps.
An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications, Vol. I 10/10 My favorite book. Feller's exposition flows unbelievably well and the exercises are rich. Majorly underappreciated — no book comes close. Became quite adept at combinatorics working through all the exercises here.
Linear Statistical Inference and Its Applications 7/10 Rao does a phenomenal job making it self-contained. The sections on probability distributions shine. Never made it through the actual statistics sections — read for the probability and math, skim the statistics as interest dictates.
All of Statistics 5/10 Parametric inference and hypothesis testing sections are not bad. Otherwise a boring read with exercises disconnected from applications, and exposition that reads like disjoint Wikipedia pages.
Statistical Models: Theory and Practice 7/10 Great for econometrics and sociology. Good intuition, talks through i.i.d. assumptions and alternatives, right balance of theory and practice. That said, most practitioners rely on hard-won experience over this theory now.
Calcul Différentiel 6/10 Great read, organized like Rudin's series on Analysis. The book cover makes it difficult to hold without damaging the spine.
Art of Computer Programming: Sorting and Searching 5.5/10 Love the rated exercises and solutions. Loathe the exposition — boring, unclear, and too reliant on citations that derail the flow. Didn't finish but read and did enough to have a view.
Principles of Operations Research 6.5/10 Techniques are outdated but surprisingly readable and grounded in real applications — sales forecasting, sensitivity testing. Interesting and rich with examples.
Advanced Calculus 7/10 Complete, rigorous, well-organized. Harder than Rudin or Bartle but more comprehensive — nice extensions into manifolds, exterior calculus, and differential equations. Too dense for a semester; fine for a full year.
Network Flows ?/10 Recommended by a friend who's a rockstar in the space. Abstract and hard to approach for self-study. Still unsure if it'll just accumulate dust.
Time Series Analysis 6/10 Dense and encyclopedic — the ARIMA sections are challenging but not very insightful. Equation-heavy in the worst way: read an equation, derive the next, find a disjointed reference, repeat. Feels clunky (so does time series).
Scheduling: Theory, Algorithms, and Systems 5/10 Love the topic, dislike the book. Purely academic with little applications for a subject entirely dependent on applications. Poisson and Exponential flow assumptions feel useless in practice.
Probabilistic Method 6.5/10 Alon is a GOAT and the perfect person to explain this. Love the examples but the exercises feel sparse and some proofs are terse — not always from the book, as Paul would say.
Naive Set Theory 7/10 Halmos is a master expositor. Mathematicians born in the early 1900s had a gift for clarity — simple, elegant, approachable. The kind of writing that demands proper attention spans.
Theoretical Statistics 5/10 Like Rudin for statistics. The dryness is crushing and the motivation hard to find. Statistics is meaningless without another discipline and this text doesn't instill that mindset at all.
Principles of Combinatorics 6/10 Derives counting tactics methodically and playfully. Big gap: no exercises. Could be a fantastic student text with them.
Your Stone Age Brain in the Screen Age 7/10 Explains why spending a working life staring at screens isn't great for mental health or productivity. A bit long for the idea (as most books in the genre are) but the message lands.
1984 8.5/10 Incredibly powerful and prescient. The ending destroyed me — the most devastating of any book I've read. We're in eerie proximity to this world now.
The Great Gatsby 7/10 My all-time favorite school read. Fitzgerald's prose is extraordinary and his commentary on the American dream stuck with me. The film's ending is the right one.
To Kill a Mockingbird 7.5/10 A beautiful book with a powerful message. Atticus Finch is one of the great characters in American fiction.
Lord of the Flies 6/10 Strong main plot. The violence toward the end — even as commentary on the duality of mankind — was a bit much.
Of Mice and Men 7.5/10 Lennie and the plot are wonderful. The ending drew a tear — reminds me of having to put down an old dog before a more painful end takes them anyway.
Crime and Punishment 4/10 A more sophisticated language than I was ready for. Read via audiobook, which I don't recommend. Can barely recall the plot.
The Stranger 4/10 Wrote several essays on it but the only thing I remember is a deeply indifferent narrator.
Brave New World 5/10 Similar commentary to 1984 on censorship and world-states. Less memorable for me.
Fahrenheit 451 6/10 Book burning and the eerie proximity to current events (2026) make this one hit differently than when I first read it.
One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest 7.5/10 Read completely and thoroughly enjoyed it. Nurse Ratched is one of the great literary characters. Kesey's critique of institutional control over the mentally ill lands hard.
The Giver 7/10 A blur — I mix memories of this and 1984. The role reminds me of an academic growing isolated in their own bubble of expertise. Schools prescribe too many depressing reads.
Atlas Shrugged 5/10 Read it before judging. I love Dagny's devotion to craft. But the book praises a kind of survival-of-the-fittest genocide and conflates capitalist self-interest with merit. Far too long as well.
Meditations 6/10 Overhyped — a few gems buried in confusing, hard-to-decipher writing. Wishes these observations were grounded in more of his actual experience.
Moby Dick 6/10 Never finished. Beautiful sea descriptions. The perplexity is high and ADHD made it hard to sustain beyond five pages at a time.
Hunter x Hunter 9/10 One of my favorite shōnen - Togashi created such a fun universe of characters and I hope the Dark Continent gets written before he's done forever.
Demon Slayer 7/10 The story is surprisingly much less exciting than the anime - wow are the graphics plus story much more fleshed out and interesting.
Monster 7.5/10 The story was unbelievably addicting. Hook after hook - Urasawa wrote an epic thriller here and it was so fun to follow but it got a bit drawn out at the end and the conclusion was unsatisfying.
