Some daily coffee thoughts.

coffee ideas

  • roast largely determines body (ranging from dark - heavy/full body to light - acidic)
  • espresso amplifies the top note and hides the others; really interesting if done right
  • dark roasts give more caramel/round flavors whereas light roasts are acidic/sour (on the extreme ends) for espresso
  • temperature and the amount of grounds are more knobs for body; temp controls altitude and grounds control mean/median of the body
  • if temperature is too high, metalic/astringent notes kick in - really depends on roast/bean and processing
  • new coffees bloom with more CO2 causing the bed to agitate/move more than usual; if you coarsen grind, you can sidestep this but at risk of a hollow cup
  • pouring speed matters a lot in bloom phase; if too fast, bed deepens and collapses causing uneven extraction of grounds; if too slow, some grounds get extracted before others - same effect interestingly enough
  • never ever put a pourover in paper if you own a speciality shop... just don't - it tastes horrible and heat causes paper smell to permeate in coffee experience
  • if the grind is too coarse, coffee velocity through coffee is too fast and cup is hollow; if too fine, coffee doesn't percolate through bed and instead moves out side of dripper during bloom phase initially causing uneven/heavy notes to creep in
  • temperature and grind size are extremely correlated independent variables - I recommend fixing one and tweaking the other
  • with grind size, you clearly see finer grinds result in higher % of the coffee being extracted BUT the taste gets more dark/roasty/chocolately and you risk bitter notes creeping in
  • coarse grinds produce light cups and do not bloom much; when coffee is newer, this is my go-to as the newer beans already tend to rupture on bloom and move around so this stabilizes the bed more and keeps the cup underextracted but smooth/light
