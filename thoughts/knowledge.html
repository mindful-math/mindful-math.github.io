Some comments on knowledge, consumerism, and my experiences.

consumerism

Consumerism... is characterized by the continuous acquisition of goods and services in ever-increasing quantities. In contemporary consumer society, the purchase and the consumption of products have evolved beyond the mere satisfaction of basic human needs, transforming into an activity that is not only economic but also cultural, social, and even identity-forming [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumerism).

Knowledge is an awareness of facts, a familiarity with individuals and situations, or a practical skill. Knowledge of facts, also called propositional knowledge, is often characterized as true belief that is distinct from opinion or guesswork by virtue of justification [[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge).

  • I do not know why but science has devolved into an extreme form of consumerism.
  • CLIP, Flamingo, Gemini, CogVLM, InternVL, GPT-4V, PaLM-E, AGD, Adagrad, SGD, SGD with Momentum, etc.
  • At the end of the day, these ideas take time to learn / consume and rarely help us in our day to day life or make us feel any bit more satiated with life.
  • But corporations are modern day plantations: no one is dying now of hunger (nor has to), there is enough livable spaces to house everyone equitably, and yet we keep consuming and gunning for "progression" and betterment. This artificially induces competition and drives us further from inner peace and contentment.
  • People retire largely because they end up jaded with consumption - they feel satiated with what they have or disgusted with themselves for what they have become and I wish we could come up with a solution to maximize everyone's well-being as opposed to listening to the minority that treat us like pawns or lines in a spreadsheet.

What would science look like if it was not driven by consumerism?

Science is a systematic discipline that builds and organises knowledge in the form of testable hypotheses and predictions about the universe [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science)

It is inherently based on pushing boundaries albeit at increasingly lower rates of return. At some point (a point I believe already occurred), we are beyond the "mere satisfaction of basic human needs" and spoiling ourselves by sounding smart and writing yet another pretentious paper about some obscure topic that will not help anyone.

The inherent issue we face is that of deciding value. If we already have the materials needed to be equible, then how do we allocate these resources. It could be the loudest that gets them or the ones that complain the most or the youth or the old or all sorts of measures that have pros and cons. If we look at related species of animals like lions or bears, they face similar challenges with allocating sparse resources to larger communities where there are established hierarchies for who gets what. Even for plant-eating species, we see similar hierarchies being formed [[4]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4s2986k7/qt4s2986k7noSplash0bedf44311d1ddee319a4a400e94c280.pdf). There are submissive and dominant sub-cultures that are created and end up deciding how to allocate resources.

Must we form hierarchies?

The answer depends on if we have a unified purpose and no longer maintain an ego. Biological examples include schools or fish or larvae where their survival depends on them acting unconsciously as a group to maintain order. Is this better or worse? I have no freaking clue, but enlightened folk also lose their sense of self or ego and so I wonder if it is indeed possible in the first place to construct such a world. Again, would this be better or worse - I have no clue! I also wonder if this society becomes like a dampened wave where lows and highs are masqueraded whereas our society features lots of lows and highs throughout life and this roller coaster may make for better / more interesting stories at the expense of a select few unlucky folk. Interesting questions but I have to return to work lol...
