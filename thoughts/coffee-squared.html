more coffee

2026-09-02

Some thoughts on coffee + pottery after trip.

  • For most coffees, earthy + brutalist colors make the most sense for mugs
  • Bright colors like RGBIV might be interesting to emphasize certain colors of fruits in cups but color is a bit offputting to contrast w/ brown color
  • Ceramics are great for low-temp cups (e.g. heavily processed cups / anaerobics)
  • Copper/iron/steel/other metals are great for keeping a cup high temp (e.g. washed/naturals that need high heat and do not change flavor profile nicely when cooled down)
  • Wood is interesting (have yet to experiment with or see in any good shops though)
  • Paper is an absolute crime; shops that use this for non-iced cups are just wrong - heat + paper seeps into flavor and you end up tasting paper and much less of the sensoral/flavor notes
  • Glass is the best probably for assessing flavor objectively (transparency prevents you from presuming flavors)
  • Plastics are probably not good for your gut health ;) - avoid
  • Porcelain only works with iced or cooled coffees but the touch/light feel is very good for tea/fruity cups

TLDR: I think ceramics are best if you care about the shape/figure of the cup + you can change the color to match/enhance taste notes. However... glass is probably the most practical and utilitarian for spanning all flavors equally well.
