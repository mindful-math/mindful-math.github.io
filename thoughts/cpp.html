A review of the book by Torsten Will titled "C++: The Comprehensive Guide".

general

2.5/5. It reads terribly — not intended to be read cover to cover (as I did this past month). Some parts are quite useful regarding modern C++ syntax and details, but most is just like AI/NPC-generated text on concepts you can find in more detail by skimming the docs. He covers statements and control flow in so much detail, yet testing and templating are left as exercises for the reader (not really, but it felt like it). It honestly reads like a bunch of blog posts strung together by a busy engineer (oh wait, that's what I do...) and is not the quality I hoped for — no magnum opus. I wish he would have interjected with more exercises for readers (to test if they are listening) and dropped the dumb tables and code samples in favor of more professional and polished ones or links to repos (I don't need to see Fibonacci numbers every 10 pages). Oh, and I was also interested to see how he would cover threading — it was painfully boring and lacking in detail. I may recommend beginner C++ engineers skim through it to discover topics, but even then, there are so many more high-quality C++ references online and by the creators themselves...
