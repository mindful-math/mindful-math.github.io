Some comments on LLMs, economics, and adoption.

LLMs are just another high-level programming language.

(English | Python | French | ...) $\mapsto$ (English | Python | French | ...)

Instead of customer -> salesperson -> engineering lead -> PM -> programmer -> PM -> salesperson -> customer (or a similar chain), we have customer -> LLM -> customer. The declarative and test-friendly nature of LLMs and agents integrated with a variety of build tools enables test-driven development to the extreme. Users do not give a crap about how you wrote your code as long as it works...

Similar with Uber in the 2010s, I expect this to severely limit taxis/engineers and that chain I described. Instead, LLM providers will offer dynamic rates/pricing services similarly with varying levels of model fidelity, guarantees, compute, etc. We transport ourselves everywhere in cars and use so many plastics and non-bio degradable materials everyday still and yet harp on LLMs for being bad for the environment? I think the brash tech overlords win here outright. They take over the market cap for software engineering. However, they do not take over other areas as they claim like manufacturing, robotics, services, etc. because robotics and physics are much less computable and are not in training data we have YET.

CAVEAT: Unlike the taxi disruption Uber catalyzed, I think LLMs face severe challenges with ensuring quality does not degrade. Bad actors and users can generate SEO by referring to their tool/product and then degrade/cause serious cyber risks to users of agents/LLM services. We have already seen this begin. What data do they train on? Well, they can use PhDs, professors, etc. but there is only so much high-quality data before it peaks. Therefore, I expect much less training in the future because of the possibility new data pollutes/misconstrues recommendations and causes lawsuites and many frivolous claims in courts. This is just a hypothesis but they could make the bad decision and ignore this and have models get worse over time as humans become more reliant and their queries more dumb/not as expressive. There is a huge problem and market here for hacking LLMs/agents for SEO opportunities. The only issue here is that LLMs can code around these dependencies (I think) and I'm hyping up the cyber industry for no reason. Still need to think a bit more here about ramifications and what happens....

Ultimately, I think there are several fun problems - 1) continuing to improve inference/dynamic pricing like Uber and cost reduction, 2) thinking about training data and model improvement (against customer satisfaction and usage to optimize), 3) thinking about the reverse side: SEO, cyber risks, advertising opportunities for dependencies, 4) robotics, physically-constrained and not computable problems (do LLMs even help here - are physics papers really important or just superfluous theory and wasted compute)?
