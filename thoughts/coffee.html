coffee roasts

2026-07-24

A review of coffee shops and coffee resources.

coffee ideas

  • Acidity is interesting; darker roasts drop acidity, which is NOT a bad thing. I actually love a tea-like, low-acidity cup with a silky body (achieved with a slightly darker roast).
  • I do not like acidic espressos (light roast or extra-light roasts); espressos amplify flavor and make it quite sour.
  • For pour-overs, I expected to like all fruity cups but learned I like them in light-bodied coffees; I had a heavier-bodied red-fruit cup that became a bit too wine-like and nauseating to drink.

coffee recipes

https://sca.coffee/store

  • The flavor wheel here is quite useful; I wish I had tabulated all the coffees I have tasted.
  • The cupping attributes are quite nice when thinking about how to formulate opinions on coffee taste as well.
  • I see this in a lot of shops (not many great ones, though...)

https://www.baristahustle.com/coffee-compass/

  • I have seen this in a few shops.
  • I am upset that it removes a few dimensions: temperature and pouring speed/structure.
  • Temperature affects the body, and if coffee is heavily processed, metallic flavors creep in and make the cup terrible.
  • Besides that, I think it is useful to remind yourself about what you can do.

https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLzDmvPifPhgpXKH4OVL5JhXzKgEsOCrhD

  • April Coffee recipes are great; I prefer the 2-pour method when possible.
  • They don't post often.

https://www.youtube.com/@yukiscoffeeuniversity/videos

  • Yuki's Coffee University is a great resource for all kinds of coffee drinks.
  • He has great attention to detail and doesn't waste words on meaningless chatter.
  • He doesn't post often.

https://www.youtube.com/@MrTsaoo

  • He has a great video on pour-over blooming and extraction; this is one of my favorites.
  • He doesn't post often.
